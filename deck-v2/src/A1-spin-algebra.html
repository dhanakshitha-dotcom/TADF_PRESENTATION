Two-spin states
\lvert S\rangle=\frac{\lvert{\uparrow\downarrow}\rangle-\lvert{\downarrow\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\lvert T_0\rangle=\frac{\lvert{\uparrow\downarrow}\rangle+\lvert{\downarrow\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}\lvert T_{+}\rangle=\lvert{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle,\qquad\lvert T_{-}\rangle=\lvert{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle

Uncorrelated formation with equal weights gives P(S) = ¼, P(T) = ¾. This is a model for electrical excitation, not a universal rule for every way of exciting a molecule.

Why T₁ → S₀ is forbidden
\Psi=\psi_{\mathrm{spatial}}\,\chi_{\mathrm{spin}}
\langle\Psi_f\vert\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}\vert\Psi_i\rangle=\langle\psi_f\vert\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}\vert\psi_i\rangle\,\langle\chi_f\vert\chi_i\rangle
\langle\chi_S\vert\chi_{T_0}\rangle=\tfrac12(1-1)=0

The dipole operator acts on space and leaves spin alone, so orthogonal spin functions give a vanishing matrix element. Spin–orbit coupling mixes singlet and triplet character and opens the transition weakly.
